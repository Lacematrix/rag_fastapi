### P1

Let $X = [x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4]$ be the data matrix:

$$X = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 & -1 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$

The uncentered covariance matrix $A$ is:

$$A = \frac{1}{4}XX^T = \frac{1}{4}\begin{bmatrix} 3 & 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 1 & 2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & -1 & 2 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 0.75 & 0.25 & -0.25 & -0.25 \\ 0.25 & 0.5 & -0.25 & 0 \\ -0.25 & -0.25 & 0.5 & 0 \\ -0.25 & 0 & 0 & 0.25 \end{bmatrix}$$

#### (a) i.

We set $\nu = 1$ and the initial vector $v_0 = [1, 0, 0, 0]^T$.

The shifted matrix system is $(A - I)w_k = v_{k-1}$. To avoid decimals during manual elimination, we can equivalently solve $4(A - I)w_k = 4v_{k-1}$, where:

$$4(A - I) = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & 1 & -1 & -1 \\ 1 & -2 & -1 & 0 \\ -1 & -1 & -2 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 & -3 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Iteration 1:**

Solve $4(A - I)w_1 = 4v_0 = [4, 0, 0, 0]^T$.

Using Gaussian elimination, we find the unnormalized vector:

$$w_1 = \begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \\ -3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix}$$

Normalizing $w_1$ by its $L_2$ norm ($\|w_1\|_2 = \sqrt{9+9+9+1} = \sqrt{28}$):

$$v_1 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{28}}\begin{bmatrix} 3 \\ 3 \\ -3 \\ -1 \end{bmatrix} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.5669 \\ 0.5669 \\ -0.5669 \\ -0.1890 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Iteration 2:**

Solve $4(A - I)w_2 = 4v_1 = \frac{4}{\sqrt{28}}[3, 3, -3, -1]^T$.

Solving the system yields:

$$w_2 = \frac{4}{\sqrt{28}}\begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 4 \\ -4 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix}$$

Normalizing $w_2$ by its norm ($\| [7, 4, -4, -2]^T \|_2 = \sqrt{85}$):

$$v_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{85}}\begin{bmatrix} 7 \\ 4 \\ -4 \\ -2 \end{bmatrix} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.7593 \\ 0.4339 \\ -0.4339 \\ -0.2169 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Iteration 3:**

Solve $4(A - I)w_3 = 4v_2 = \frac{4}{\sqrt{85}}[7, 4, -4, -2]^T$.

Solving the system yields:

$$w_3 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{85}}\begin{bmatrix} 47 \\ 31 \\ -31 \\ -13 \end{bmatrix}$$

Normalizing $w_3$ by its norm ($\| [47, 31, -31, -13]^T \|_2 = \sqrt{4300}$):

$$v_3 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{4300}}\begin{bmatrix} 47 \\ 31 \\ -31 \\ -13 \end{bmatrix} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.7167 \\ 0.4727 \\ -0.4727 \\ -0.1982 \end{bmatrix}$$

### **(a) ii. Second Principal Component (Shift $\nu = 0.4$)**

We reset the shift to $\nu = 0.4$ with the initial vector $v_0 = [1, 0, 0, 0]^T$.

The system is $(A - 0.4I)w_k = v_{k-1}$. Multiplying by 20 simplifies the coefficient matrix:

$$20(A - 0.4I) = \begin{bmatrix} 7 & 5 & -5 & -5 \\ 5 & 2 & -5 & 0 \\ -5 & -5 & 2 & 0 \\ -5 & 0 & 0 & -3 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Iteration 1:**

Solve $20(A - 0.4I)w_1 = 20v_0 = [20, 0, 0, 0]^T$.

Solving the system yields the exact unnormalized direction:

$$w_1 = \frac{5}{43}\begin{bmatrix} 21 \\ -15 \\ 15 \\ -35 \end{bmatrix}$$

Normalizing $w_1$ by its norm ($\| [21, -15, 15, -35]^T \|_2 = \sqrt{2116} = 46$):

$$v_1 = \frac{1}{46}\begin{bmatrix} 21 \\ -15 \\ 15 \\ -35 \end{bmatrix} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.4565 \\ -0.3261 \\ 0.3261 \\ -0.7609 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Iteration 2:**

Solve $20(A - 0.4I)w_2 = 20v_1 = \frac{20}{46}[21, -15, 15, -35]^T$.

Solving the linear system yields:

$$w_2 = \frac{1}{43}\begin{bmatrix} 529 \\ -470 \\ 470 \\ -380 \end{bmatrix}$$

Normalizing $w_2$ by its norm ($\sqrt{866041}$):

$$v_2 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{866041}}\begin{bmatrix} 529 \\ -470 \\ 470 \\ -380 \end{bmatrix} \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.5684 \\ -0.5050 \\ 0.5050 \\ -0.4083 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Iteration 3:**

Solve $20(A - 0.4I)w_3 = 20v_2$.

Following the exact fractional bounds mapping from the previous iteration, the unnormalized direction resolves to:

$$w_3 \propto \begin{bmatrix} 269563 \\ -273385 \\ 273385 \\ -296765 \end{bmatrix}$$

Normalizing this vector provides the 3rd iteration approximation:

$$v_3 \approx \begin{bmatrix} 0.4840 \\ -0.4908 \\ 0.4908 \\ -0.5328 \end{bmatrix}$$

### **(a) iii. Mean Square Error onto the First Two PCs**

A foundational property of PCA is that the Mean Square Error of projecting data onto the first $k$ principal components is exactly equal to the sum of the remaining (discarded) eigenvalues of the covariance matrix.

Let the eigenvalues of the uncentered covariance matrix $A$ be $\mu_1 \ge \mu_2 \ge \mu_3 \ge \mu_4$.

First, we find the exact eigenvalues of $A$ by solving the characteristic equation $\det(A - \mu I) = 0$. Since $A = \frac{1}{4}XX^T$, we can instead find the eigenvalues $\lambda$ of $XX^T$ and use $\mu_k = \frac{\lambda_k}{4}$.

The characteristic polynomial of $XX^T$ expands to:

$$\lambda^4 - 8\lambda^3 + 19\lambda^2 - 16\lambda + 4 = 0$$

Factoring this equation gives:

$$(\lambda - 1)(\lambda - 2)(\lambda^2 - 5\lambda + 2) = 0$$

The roots (eigenvalues of $XX^T$) are:

- $\lambda_1 = \frac{5 + \sqrt{17}}{2} \approx 4.5615$
- $\lambda_2 = 2$
- $\lambda_3 = 1$
- $\lambda_4 = \frac{5 - \sqrt{17}}{2} \approx 0.4385$

Dividing by 4 yields the exact eigenvalues of our covariance matrix $A$:

- $\mu_1 = \frac{5 + \sqrt{17}}{8}$
- $\mu_2 = 0.5$
- $\mu_3 = 0.25$
- $\mu_4 = \frac{5 - \sqrt{17}}{8}$

The theoretical MSE of the projection onto the first two true principal components is the sum of the remaining eigenvalues:

$$MSE = \mu_3 + \mu_4 = 0.25 + \frac{5 - \sqrt{17}}{8}$$

$$MSE = \frac{2}{8} + \frac{5 - \sqrt{17}}{8} = \frac{7 - \sqrt{17}}{8}$$

$$MSE \approx 0.3596$$
